# Cash Box — Demo Voiceover Script

*Read this aloud while clicking through the prototype. The lines in **[square brackets]** are
stage directions — what to click — not meant to be read out. Everything else is the spoken
script, written in simple English.*

**Total run time:** about 4–5 minutes.

---

## 0 · Opening (before you click anything)

> "Hi. In this short demo I'll show you the new Cash Box module — the tool that replaces the
> weekly caja process.
>
> Right now, that process is fully manual. Field teams abroad photograph their receipts,
> upload them to SharePoint, and type every expense into an Excel by hand. Then three people
> in Madrid check each line one by one before any money is released.
>
> We're replacing all of that with one simple flow, inside the same app you already use — same
> login, no separate tool.
>
> There are two kinds of people in this flow. On one side, the **Field Director** and their
> sales reps — the people on the ground who spend the money and upload receipts. On the other
> side, the **Finance Admin** in Madrid — the person who sends the money, checks the expenses,
> and approves them.
>
> Let me walk you through both sides."

---

## 1 · Finance Admin — setting up a project

> "Let's start with Finance, because everything begins here."

**[Click the "Finance Admin" button at the top right.]**

> "This is the Finance Admin's home screen. Paula, in Madrid, can see all her active projects
> in one place — how much money was sent to each, how much has been spent, the balance left,
> and how many expenses are waiting for her to check.
>
> Everything starts with a project. Let me create one."

**[Click "New project".]**

> "A project is always one country — that's a tax requirement. So Paula gives it a name, for
> example 'India — The Guardian 2026'. She picks the country, chooses whether the director is
> paid in dollars or euros, and sets the exchange rate. That rate is the director's own rate,
> and the system never changes it.
>
> Once the project exists, she adds the team — one Field Director, who is responsible for the
> money, and any sales reps who help upload receipts."

**[Close this window.]**

---

## 2 · Finance Admin — sending the money

**[Click into the "India · The Guardian" project from the sidebar or the table.]**

> "Here's the project itself. The first thing Paula does is send the director some money to
> start with. Let's say a thousand dollars."

**[Click "Send funds".]**

> "She enters the amount and, importantly, attaches a photo of the transfer as proof. Only
> Finance can send money and record it — the director can never do this himself. This keeps
> the money side fully controlled.
>
> Once she saves it, that thousand dollars becomes the opening balance."

**[Close this window. Point to the balance ledger below.]**

> "And you can see it right here in the balance ledger. Every bit of money — money coming in,
> and every approved expense going out — is listed in order, with the running balance on the
> right. Nothing is hidden. This is the full, honest history of the project's cash."

---

## 3 · Field Director — spending and uploading receipts

> "Now let's switch to the other side — the field director on the ground in India."

**[Click "Field Director" at the top right.]**

> "This is Rahul's view. He only sees his own projects — nothing from Finance, nothing from
> other teams. He taps into his project."

**[Click into the "India · The Guardian" project.]**

> "He can see how much money he received, how much he's spent, and what's left. He can also see
> his spending broken down by category.
>
> Now — he takes a taxi. He pays for it. And instead of writing anything down, he just uploads
> the receipt right away, from his phone."

**[Click "Upload receipt".]**

> "He takes a photo of the receipt — or a screenshot, or a PDF, whatever he has."

**[Click "Simulate upload & read receipt".]**

> "And this is the clever part. The AI reads the receipt for him. It pulls out the amount, the
> date, what it was for, the reference number, and it even suggests the right category.
>
> If something on the receipt is blurry or unclear, the system highlights it and asks him to
> check it — you can see one field highlighted here. It never makes up a number. If it can't
> read something important, it asks, rather than guessing.
>
> Rahul just checks it looks right and logs the expense."

**[Click "Log expense".]**

> "That's it. The expense is now logged and sent to Finance for review. No Excel, no typing, no
> SharePoint folders. This whole thing takes a few seconds."

---

## 4 · Finance Admin — reviewing and catching problems

> "So now the expense lands back with Paula in Madrid. Let's see what she sees."

**[Click "Finance Admin", then click "Review queue" in the sidebar.]**

> "This is the review queue — every expense the team has logged, waiting for her decision. And
> this is where the tool really saves time.
>
> For each expense, she sees the original receipt on the left, and the information the AI pulled
> out on the right, side by side. She doesn't have to open folders or retype anything.
>
> And most importantly, the system flags anything that looks off — automatically."

**[Point to the first item — the hotel.]**

> "Here, for example — this hotel is right at the limit of the nightly budget, and the stay
> covers a weekend. The system flags both, so Paula knows to check them."

**[Point to the second item — the PR event.]**

> "This one is a PR event. That type of expense needs approval from Rafa or Mary before it can
> be paid, and there's no approval on file — so it's flagged in red as high priority."

**[Point to the third item — the duplicate taxi.]**

> "And this taxi — the system noticed it's the same amount and the same route as another taxi
> already logged. It might be a duplicate. So it flags it for Paula to double-check.
>
> Nothing is ever rejected automatically. The system only highlights — a person always makes
> the final decision."

> "For a genuine expense, Paula simply approves it."

**[Click "Verify & deduct" on one item.]**

> "The moment she approves it, the amount is automatically taken off the balance. She never has
> to do any maths.
>
> And if something's wrong, she rejects it with a reason."

**[Click "Reject with reason" on another item, then send it.]**

> "She writes why — for example, 'this looks like a duplicate, please re-upload with the time
> visible'. That reason goes straight back to the director."

---

## 5 · Field Director — fixing a rejection

> "Let's quickly see what the director gets when something is rejected."

**[Click "Field Director", go into the project, scroll to "Needs your attention".]**

> "Right here, Rahul sees exactly what was rejected and why, in plain words. He can fix it and
> re-upload in one tap. No emails, no back-and-forth on Basecamp — it's all in one place."

---

## 6 · Analytics — the big picture

> "Finally, everything the team spends turns into useful numbers."

**[Click "Finance Admin", then "Analytics".]**

> "Finance gets a clear picture across all projects — total spending, how much is going to each
> category, which projects are spending the most, and how many expenses got flagged. What used
> to be buried in dozens of Excel files is now visible at a glance."

---

## 7 · Closing

> "So, to sum up.
>
> The director spends money and uploads a receipt from his phone — that's it for him. The AI
> reads it. Finance sees a clean, organised list, gets warned about anything unusual, and
> approves or rejects with one click. The balance updates itself, and every step is recorded.
>
> It replaces the SharePoint folders, the manual Excel typing, and the line-by-line checking —
> with one simple flow that everyone shares.
>
> This is just the first module. The same login and the same system will carry the editorial
> tools you already have, and future modules too. Thank you."

---

### Quick tips for recording
- Speak slowly and pause between scenes — give the screen a second to catch up.
- The **top-right switch** flips between the two people. You'll use it four times.
- If you want a shorter version, you can skip Section 1 (creating a project) and Section 6
  (analytics) and still tell the whole story.
