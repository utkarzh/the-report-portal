TRC Retorno Strategy Playbook
Prepared by Rafa, 14.11.2026 - Internal
1. Objective
The objective of every follow-up is to advance a genuine commercial opportunity towards a signed agreement.
The Sales Executive must decide on a follow-up strategy immediately after any meeting that does not result in a contract. Speed is essential. Never simply wait for the CEO or marketing department to get back to you. Decide within hours, including when the right decision is not to follow up.
The actions described below are tactical options, not a mandatory sequence. Executives may use one, several or a combination simultaneously, depending on the meeting, the level of interest and their commercial judgment.
2. Assess Commercial Interest Before Acting
Before selecting follow-up actions, consider:
• Did the CEO demonstrate genuine commercial interest?
• Were specific formats, spaces and prices discussed?
• Did the CEO express a preference or endorse participation?
• Who is responsible for taking the decision forward?
• What commitments or next steps were agreed?
Strong interest: Act quickly, using the tactics most likely to secure the agreement.
Speed is mandatory in every case: act quickly, whether the decision is to pursue a follow-up or deliberately not to pursue one.
Uncertain interest: Use targeted, low-effort actions to clarify whether the opportunity is worth pursuing.
Little or no interest: Send a short standard proposal if appropriate, then move on. Do not consume valuable time and resources pursuing an opportunity without meaningful buying signals.
Ideally, a clear rejection should already have been established during the commercial presentation, eliminating unnecessary follow-up.
3. Follow-Up Tactics
3.1. Proposal Email + Media Kit
Effort: Low.
When it works best: The CEO has shown interest, and specific commercial options have been discussed. Marketing may participate in implementation or approval.
Send a short, concrete email to the CEO, copying marketing where appropriate, and attach the standard media kit.
The CEO should understand the offer at first glance: what is being proposed, what is included and how much it costs.
Avoid elaborate PowerPoint presentations unless there is a specific reason to prepare one. They take time, may not be read by the CEO and can encourage unnecessary delegation.
The email should:
• Refer briefly to the meeting.
• State the specific format or formats discussed.
• Show the price of each option clearly.
• List the principal benefits in a telegraphic format.
• Attach the media kit.
• Propose a concrete next step where appropriate.
Do not write lengthy introductions praising the CEO, repeating the interview or explaining everything about the company.
Example
Dear Mr. Madiyev,
It was a pleasure meeting you yesterday. Our team is already preparing the interview transcript.
As discussed, I am sharing the proposed options for Astana Hub's participation in our upcoming Kazakhstan business special in USA Today.
Corporate Profile — 1/4 Page
Print and digital eMag: USD 17,000
Includes:
• Corporate profile in print and digital
• Link to Astana Hub's digital properties
• Full Q&A on our website
• Podcast on Spotify and Apple Podcasts
Please find the media kit attached.
Your interview transcript and initial visual layouts should be ready on Friday morning.
Would you have 10 minutes on Friday before I leave for Almaty to review the interview content?
Kind regards,
Maria

3.2. Interview Transcript
Effort: Low. Generated through TRC's AI platform.
When it works best: The interview transcript is a key editorial document and tactical tool in every CEO relationship. CEOs have agreed to the interview because they value it. Use it to reinforce engagement, demonstrate professionalism, provide editorial value and create opportunities for further contact.
The interview is the editorial value we bring to the CEO. A strong transcript demonstrates the quality of the messages conveyed and encourages the CEO to consider their wider exposure.
Possible approaches include:
• Send the transcript directly by WhatsApp or email.
• Use it to arrange another short meeting with the CEO.
• Send it to marketing, keeping the CEO copied, to facilitate the next discussion.
• Combine it with a commercial proposal when appropriate.
Example WhatsApp
Hi Mr. Madiyev, here is the first draft of our interview transcript. It contains some excellent messages. Please feel free to review it and flag any factual inaccuracies. Best regards, Maria.
Notice that nothing is requested. The objective is to provide editorial value and reinforce the relationship.
Send transcripts within hours, if possible, when appropriate. Time is of the essence. Do not withhold them for weeks while trying to secure another meeting. A delay should only occur when there is a concrete strategic reason.
3.3. Corporate Profile Layout
Effort: Moderate. Requires coordination with the graphic design department, although existing layouts can be adapted.
When it works best: The CEO has demonstrated meaningful interest, and a visual representation of the proposed coverage could strengthen engagement or facilitate internal discussions.
The transcript demonstrates the quality of the CEO's messages. The layout demonstrates how the coverage could be presented.
The Sales Executive should provide the design department with suitable company images, CEO photographs and relevant links.
The transcript and layout can be sent together or separately.
Two touchpoints may be more valuable than one, particularly when a marketing meeting is approaching. They give the CEO additional opportunities to engage with the content and potentially express support internally.
Example WhatsApp
Mr. Madiyev, here are two initial visual layouts showing how we envisage Astana Hub's coverage in USA Today. We will also review them with your team. Best regards, Maria.
Do not unnecessarily delay the transcript while waiting for the layout.
A layout is an optional commercial tactic, not a requirement for every follow-up.
3.4. Meeting with Marketing
When it works best: The CEO supports participation but wants marketing involved in taking the agreement forward.
The objective is to build on the CEO's endorsement, not restart the entire sales process.
Ideally, the Sales Executive has already established the CEO's preferred space, price or commercial direction.
Open the marketing meeting by referring to the CEO’s involvement and the options discussed. Being able to do this demonstrates that the Sales Executive successfully narrowed down the commercial options during the planteo.
Example
"We are producing a business special on Kazakhstan in USA Today. Mr. Madiyev and I discussed the opportunity for Astana Hub to participate through a dedicated corporate profile at USD 17,000. He responded positively and asked me to discuss the next steps with you. How shall we proceed?"
The marketing team must understand that the CEO is already involved.
This does not mean refusing to explain the product, answer questions or discuss implementation. It means approaching the meeting as a continuation of the CEO conversation rather than an entirely new pitch.
Never claim CEO endorsement that was not actually given. However, use every legitimate opportunity to bring the CEO’s endorsement into the conversation, supported by concrete facts from the meeting.
3.5. Second CEO Meeting or Call
When it works best: Genuine interest exists, but an obstacle, outstanding question or decision remains unresolved.
A second conversation can be used to:
• Review the transcript or layouts.
• Resolve a specific commercial objection.
• Confirm the CEO's preferred option.
• Secure the CEO's support before a marketing meeting.
• Advance the opportunity towards an agreement.
Always propose a specific meeting time and explain its purpose.
Avoid vague requests such as "When would you be available to discuss?"
Example
"As we are finalising production arrangements, I suggest we meet tomorrow at 4 pm or Thursday at 11 am to review the interview and confirm the proposed format. Would either time work for you?"
If neither works, ask for an alternative.
3.6. WhatsApp to the CEO
Effort: Very low.
When it works best: A personal connection exists and a short, direct message can maintain engagement, share editorial value or secure a specific response.
WhatsApp is primarily a relationship and coordination tool, not the place to conduct a full commercial negotiation.
Rules:
• Keep messages short, personal and professional.
• Focus on editorial value or a concrete next step.
• Avoid lengthy explanations and unnecessary praise.
• Do not negotiate prices over WhatsApp.
• Do not repeatedly push for an advertising decision.
• If requesting something, make a brief statement followed by a specific question.
WhatsApp can be particularly effective before an upcoming marketing meeting, when the CEO's engagement with the transcript or layout may strengthen internal support.
3.7. Tailored Proposal
When it works best: The meeting identified specific requirements, options or objections that cannot be adequately addressed through the standard proposal email.
Prepare a tailored commercial proposal only when it adds meaningful value. CEOs are unlikely to spend time opening lengthy PowerPoint pitches; they will often forward them to marketing. Whenever a PowerPoint is sent, include the concrete commercial offer in the body of the email, as explained above, and mention the additional presentation as an attachment.
As a general rule, TRC does not favour PowerPoint proposals. They take time to prepare and require recipients to open or download them. They can be useful as marketing materials when necessary, but the CEO must still be able to understand the full commercial offer quickly from the email itself.
Avoid elaborate presentations simply to create follow-up activity. Remember: this is YOUR time and YOUR money. Do not chase lost causes just to keep yourself busy.
3.8. Electronic Contract
When it works best: The negotiation is sufficiently advanced, there is substantial commercial interest and the executive believes presenting the agreement will help close the deal.
Prefill the agreement as much as possible.
Example wording
"For your deliberations, I am attaching our standard agreement so you can review the terms of participation. Upon a positive decision, please sign and return the agreement."
Do not send contracts prematurely when commercial interest has not been established. Doing so can appear desperate and undermine credibility.
4. Combining Tactics
There is no mandatory follow-up sequence.
For example, after a positive CEO meeting, an executive might:
• Send the interview transcript by WhatsApp within hours.
• Email the commercial proposal and media kit the same day, copying marketing.
• Send a corporate profile layout the following day.
• Attend a previously arranged marketing meeting with the CEO's endorsement clearly established.
In another case, a single proposal email may be sufficient.
The right strategy depends on the circumstances, not the number of actions taken. One tactic or several can be used simultaneously. Several well-chosen tactics can work better because they increase meaningful touchpoints. Lead with value before asking for money.
5. Rules for Effective Follow-Up
Move fast. Decide and begin executing the strategy within hours of the meeting, always. 
Never wait passively. Take the initiative in proposing the next action. Nothing will happen otherwise. 
Be concrete. Every commercial proposal must identify the actual format, benefits and price.
Keep the CEO involved. Obtain as much CEO buy-in as possible before involving marketing.
Deliver editorial value. Transcripts and layouts can reinforce engagement without requiring an immediate commercial response.
Propose specific next steps. Suggest dates and times rather than asking open-ended questions.
Communicate efficiently. Every sentence in an email or WhatsApp should serve a purpose.
Invest selectively. More substantial commercial interest justifies additional effort. Weak opportunities should not distract executives from better prospects.
Use judgment. The executive decides which tactics to use, when to use them and how to combine them.
6. The Role of AI and the Sales Executive
The Report Card's Follow-Up Decision Support should:
1. Assess the CEO's commercial interest using evidence from the meeting.
2. Recommend two or three relevant follow-up tactics, explaining why.
3. Provide access to the complete TRC Follow-Up Playbook.
4. Allow the executive to select and combine tactics, with indicative timing.
5. Offer optional guidance through Live Coach.
6. Record the final follow-up plan.
The AI supports commercial judgment. The Sales Executive remains responsible for the strategy and its execution.
